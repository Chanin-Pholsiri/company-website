# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Enterprise buyers: CTOs, VPs of Engineering, and procurement leads at mid-to-large companies evaluating software development partners. They arrive doing due diligence — comparing options, looking for signals of capability and stability. They're not easily impressed. They read carefully, skim for red flags, and make decisions based on trust, not excitement.

Primary job: assess whether this company can reliably handle something important to their business.

## Product Purpose

A template/demo software development agency website. Demonstrates what a developer-native, precision-focused agency brand looks and feels like — as a starting point or portfolio piece. Success means an enterprise visitor reads the page and concludes: "these people know what they're doing."

## Brand Personality

Precise. Rigorous. Authoritative.

Not cold — senior. The firm that doesn't need to shout. Their work, their stack choices, and their copy signal competence without claiming it. Voice is direct and specific: no buzzwords, no mission statements, no vague superlatives.

References: Linear, Vercel, Resend — developer-native precision. Clean, typographic, minimal ornament. Dark or neutral. Let hierarchy do the work.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS startup landing page**: dark bg + indigo accent + particle canvas hero + gradient text + stat cards + eyebrow labels above every section. The current site is this. It's the first-order training-data reflex for "tech agency" — recognizable within a glance. Everything that makes it generic must go.
- **Big-4 consulting beige**: stock photography of handshakes, serif headings, light gray layouts. Corporate but soulless — the other failure mode for "enterprise trust."
- **Crypto / Web3 aesthetic**: neon glow, grid overlays, dark-on-dark texture. Too much atmosphere, too little substance.

## Design Principles

1. **Craft earns trust.** Enterprise buyers judge capability by the quality of what's in front of them. Typography, spacing, and interaction precision are credentials.
2. **Type does the work.** Hierarchy and copy carry more weight than decoration. When in doubt, cut the ornament and sharpen the sentence.
3. **Specificity over claim.** "World-class" is noise. Stack choices, process details, and concrete case study outcomes are signal. Copy should describe what literally happens, not what it feels like.
4. **Restraint is a decision, not a default.** Every gradient, animation, and color layer must be justified. The current site adds visual complexity to cover conceptual thinness. Reverse that.
5. **Senior, not safe.** Precision and authority are not the same as conservative. Linear and Vercel are bold — they just commit to one thing and execute it completely.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No information conveyed by color alone. Keyboard navigable throughout.
